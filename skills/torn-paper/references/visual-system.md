# Visual system

This is the look to rebuild. It was taken from finished posters that kept a place recognizable as layered paper, not from a photo-collage recipe.

## Material

Every region is cut paper. Sky, water, stone, foliage, clothing, and skin are paper sheets with grain. Edges show a little thickness. Light is flat, as in a scan of a paper illustration. A soft shadow may appear only under an overlap.

Cream is the torn fiber. It is a narrow warm edge. It is not the color of the sky, the wall, or the ground.

## Color

Take the hues from the photograph and stay close to them. A blue sky stays blue. A red brick wall stays red. A night scene stays dark, and the lit subject stays warm. A sunset stays orange and copper.

Use several colors when the photograph has several colors. Do not collapse the picture into one bright accent, a black logo, or a beige field.

## Scale

The hero occupies about the same share of the frame as in the photograph. Buildings run to the edges when they do in the photo. A person in the foreground stays a foreground person.

Leave quiet paper only where the photograph already has a quiet field, such as open sky. Do not invent a large blank margin and shrink the place into it.

## What "recognizable" means

Keep the parts a person would use to name the photograph:

- the pose, hat, glasses, and clothing of a person
- the count and shape of towers, arches, wheels, and eaves
- a rose window, a couplet, a flag, a reflection
- which thing is in front of which

Simplify texture. Do not simplify the identity. Windows can become paper slots. A face can become a few paper planes. The result still has to be that building and that person.

## The tear

One poster has one main tear idea. The edge is irregular: some nearly straight stretches, small unequal notches, and one or two longer fiber pulls. It is not a sine wave, a row of triangles, a fuzzy white halo, or a sticker outline.

The tear separates sheets. It should be readable at thumbnail size without becoming the subject.

## Depth

Three to five sheets are enough. More sheets turn the picture into scraps. Fewer than three sheets leave no tear to read.

Order the sheets the way the photograph is ordered: far sheet, middle sheet, near sheet. A form may pass behind a tear. It may not be sliced into unrelated pieces unless that slice is the chosen event.

## People

The person is one sheet. Jaw, cheek, mouth, and the shadow under a brim are paper planes, still this person's face. No photographic skin. No second face. No extra fingers.

The tear stays outside the body. It may follow the silhouette. It may open the trees or the sky behind the person. It may split the background to either side. It may not cross the eyes, the mouth, the neck, the shirt, or the arms.

## Lettering

Add no title, caption, signature, or watermark. If the photograph has large lettering that is part of the place, keep the real words or leave the lettering out. Do not invent characters that only look like writing.

## Known misses

Regenerate a poster that looks like any of these:

- a tiny building floating in empty cream paper
- a black silhouette with one bright stripe
- a beige cardboard version of a colorful place
- a tear down a cheek, across a shirt, or through a pair of glasses
- a decorative torn oval that frames the person and the tree together, with no gap between them
- horizontal strips whose edges visually cross the body
- a photographic face pasted onto a paper body
- four recolors of one centered icon
- curled paper, a desk mockup, or a heavy drop shadow
